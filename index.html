The method

Every report · Four parts · No exceptions
I.

The Street's view

A faithful summary of the prevailing sell-side narrative, sourced from major brokerage notes. No straw men.

II.

The variant view

A precise, falsifiable claim that diverges from consensus — a specific assertion about a specific driver: utilisation, margins, deal mix, or capital allocation.

III.

The evidence

First-principles work — three-statement models, unit economics, BSE/NSE filings, concall transcripts. Every model behind a report is published alongside it, so the reader can replicate, challenge, or extend the work.

IV.

The risks

An explicit bear case: which data points would invalidate the thesis, and what signposts to watch. Analysts who cannot articulate their own risks should not be trusted.

About the analyst

Balannagari Pavan Kalyan

Variant View exists for one purpose — to identify, quantify, and pressure-test where Street consensus is wrong on Indian IT. Not earnings recaps. Not buy or sell tips. A publication whose entire editorial filter is the gap between what the sell-side believes and what the filings show.

Coverage spans the Indian IT universe — from large-caps, where genuine variant views are scarce but consequential, to mid-caps, where sell-side coverage is thinnest and the edge is sharpest. Most of the work lives in the mid-cap zone, because that is where the gap between consensus and reality is widest.
